# Judgment — integrate and decide

**Compare rivals.** Specify the claim, criteria, relevant alternatives, strongest comparison and evidence class. Preserve a frozen test's original outcome. An improvement over a weak baseline does not establish superiority over a stronger one. Prefer a test that can change the decision; if additional work adds no information or deliverable value, stop.

**Separate perspectives (PRISM).** Partition a contested decision by the actual questions, evidence classes and authority domains. Keep performance, correctness, user utility and scientific validity distinct when their criteria differ. Record shared sources and real conflicts; multiple interpretations of one source are not independent replications. No viewpoint can decide an unrelated claim merely by having standing in its own domain.

**Integrate (MOSAIC).** Align claim, scope, source and revision before combining results. Deduplicate common provenance, retain material disagreement and missing evidence. Distinguish direct observations from interpretations and new hypotheses. A derivative cannot inherit more authority than its sources support. Preserve the original records.

**Adjudicate (COURT).** Use claim-specific acceptance only when a consequential decision needs it. Distinguish permission, execution, artifact integrity, formal validity, empirical outcome, generalization and novelty. Accept a claim only within admitted evidence and criteria. Hold when required evidence is missing; reject when an applicable decisive counterexample or failed frozen criterion defeats the claim. Do not promote a build or proxy to a human or scientific outcome. Scope widening needs explicit new justification.

**Decision sensitivity.** Separate eligibility from preferences. Assess candidates on distinct 0–1 axes: problem-solving utility, quality preservation, resource efficiency, feasibility, and evidence strength. Use anchored estimates, intervals or unknowns; never collapse them into a compulsory total. Reject a violation of a required condition, retain nondominated alternatives, and explain the tradeoff that selects the next feasible action. Prefer reversible work and observations that resolve a decision-changing uncertainty. After each useful result update only affected axes and choose again; don't promote a version merely to report activity.

For an explicitly scalar preference problem only, the optional `../scripts/decision-tradeoff.mjs` compares declared weighted intervals and minimax regret. Node stdin JSON: `{criteria:["quality","speed"],profiles:[{id:"quality-first",weights:{quality:0.7,speed:0.3}}],candidates:[{id:"candidate",gate:"eligible",scores:{quality:[0.7,0.9],speed:[0.4,0.8]}}]}`. All axes higher-is-better, bounds 0–1, weights sum to one. Hold/blocked candidates are excluded. The result is conditional on the supplied profiles and independent score intervals, not a general ranking, confidence interval, or measured effect.

**Recompose (RAVEL).** When a result changes the decision, choose direct work or a different useful method. Compare expected product or research value with time, cost and burden. Repair the smallest cause, preserve necessary obligations, and distinguish a revised question from the original. Stop, park or fork when justified. No forced review after completion.

Deliver the actual useful synthesis, decision or next action with its material limits. Scores are optional estimates, not evidence or permission. No process or transport success creates scientific authority.
