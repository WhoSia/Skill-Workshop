# Inquiry — structure and discriminating tests

Use only when the research question needs more than direct reasoning.

**Formalize selectively (AXIOM).** Specify objects, domain, target, observations and admissible transformations. Locate the load-bearing assumptions; seek invariants, equivalences, obstructions and minimal examples. Distinguish proof, finite exhaustive verification, sampled consistency and conjecture. Use known theorems or structure before brute force.

**Attack what matters (LANTERN).** Fix the exact claim and construct an admissible counterexample, observationally equivalent rival, stronger baseline or negative control. A witness outside the domain does not refute the claim. Repeated attacks on the same mechanism have diminishing value; seek a different discriminating test. A failed search is not proof.

**Change representation (PIVOT).** Compare the original representation with a genuinely different one; identify preserved and lost distinctions, assumptions and a test that can discriminate. Do not change a frozen evaluation after outcomes. Missing observations require acquisition, not cosmetic reframing. A separate future design may be proposed without altering the original test.

**Adaptive discrimination.** For deterministic finite observations, two states with equal full observations but different targets prove nonidentifiability. Otherwise compare the minimum fixed set with a policy that conditions later observations on earlier results. Stop when all compatible states share the target. For worst-case positive acquisition cost, choose a splitting channel a minimizing c(a) plus the maximum remaining branch cost. Mean and worst-case cost differ; do not silently exchange them. For noisy or continuous data, establish a different inference contract.

**Discovery (SCOUT).** Search current canonical holdings first when applicable. Seek original sources and relevant competing work; expand by aliases or missing evidence, not hit count. Distinguish retrieved metadata, accessible original, admitted evidence and unresolved access. Report what a source supports and does not support.

Return the useful result or obstruction, scope, assumptions, and next decisive observation or computation. Do not invoke every method or add a new claim of novelty.
