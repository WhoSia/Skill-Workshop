# MOSAIC — candidate synthesis skill 01

Create a useful combined artifact without changing frozen sources or granting new claim authority. A better synthesis may produce a new hypothesis, but that hypothesis requires its own evidence.

## Compose around the target

- Determine the recipient's question, desired artifact and necessary distinctions. Choose the smallest set of relevant branch outputs. Keep independently useful alternatives separate when forced agreement would lose information.
- Align claim units by exact target, scope, assumptions, owner and source revision. Distinguish common original evidence from different summaries or transformations of it. Deduplicate shared ancestry without assuming distinct roots are statistically independent.
- Combine complementary information when compatible; retain disagreements, missing evidence and negative results at their original claim scope. Do not average incompatible verdicts, silently choose a preferred branch, erase HOLD, or combine engineering success with scientific authority.
- Record what is retained, narrowed, rejected, newly proposed or unresolved. A new claim or changed representation needs explicit new reasoning and, where applicable, new evidence. A source-level change must not be disguised as an editorial derivative.
- Produce the requested artifact directly, with enough provenance and uncertainty for the consequential claims to be checked. Prefer compact synthesis to mechanically concatenating all input receipts. If a conflict would change the result, return it to the owner or appropriate adjudicator before presenting a unified conclusion.
- Before reusing a prior synthesis or decision, check its actual source revisions and active justification dependencies. Unchanged root counts or eligibility alone do not establish freshness. If a trusted runtime exposes a current read-set check, use it; otherwise resolve material changes directly. Never treat a structural freshness token as scientific evidence.

## Result

Deliver the combined artifact, material conflicts, retained claim limits, source lineage and next unresolved decision. Preserve the original sources. If a required input is missing, distinguish a useful bounded partial synthesis from the complete result. Do not invent compatibility or authority.
