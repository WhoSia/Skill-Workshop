# LANTERN — candidate adversarial reasoning skill 01

Expose the failure geometry of the actual claim, without changing its scope or confusing a hypothetical case with observed evidence.

## Attack the load-bearing assumption

- Freeze the claim, quantifiers, domain, assumptions and protected invariants. Identify the weakest premise or most consequential possible error, and distinguish an attack on a claim from an attack on its measurement or evaluation procedure.
- Construct a concrete admissible witness. Prefer the smallest decisive counterexample, an observationally equivalent rival, a negative control, a boundary case or a stronger baseline as appropriate. Check that the witness satisfies the original premises and violates the stated conclusion. An out-of-domain example may suggest a new question but does not refute the original claim.
- Spend effort according to discriminating power. If several attacks expose the same obstruction, preserve a representative and the shared mechanism rather than multiplying objections. Seek a qualitatively different attack when the first does not discriminate the rivals.
- Separate demonstrated logical contradiction, exact finite-model witness, synthetic countermodel, empirical observation, and unresolved conjecture. A failed search does not prove that no counterexample exists. State the searched domain, assumptions and limitations.
- Identify what would distinguish the surviving explanations, including a stronger observation, intervention or assumption test. A useful attack changes a prediction, research action or permissible claim; stop when the next attack adds little or the budget is exhausted.
- Return the attack and its limits to the research decision. If the representation itself is the bottleneck, suggest PIVOT-style reframing; if evidence is missing, suggest targeted acquisition; if a formal claim needs repair, suggest AXIOM-style revision. Do not automatically invoke all three or issue a COURT verdict.

## Result

Report the exact target, the strongest witness or falsifier, why it is admissible, which assumption or implication fails, evidence class, unresolved attacks and the next discriminating test. Preserve frozen evaluation rules and counterexamples. Do not reinterpret a failed claim as successful after seeing results; distinguish a revised claim from its predecessor.
