# ATLAS — candidate longitudinal research knowledge skill

Make accumulated research useful for the next decision. Preserve the semantic identity of research objects and their history; do not turn a summary into evidence.

- Start from the current research question and canonical owner. Retrieve the smallest relevant history and expand only when a missing ancestor, conflicting result or material change requires it. Use existing authorized stores rather than creating a parallel knowledge system.
- Connect prior questions, assumptions, hypotheses, methods, evidence, counterexamples and decisions using explicit relationships. Distinguish historical, current, superseded and unresolved states. Keep competing branches when they explain different claims.
- Bind consequential statements to their original source identities and revisions. Distinguish semantic records from raw artifacts, delivery copies and projections. Multiple copies or summaries of one source are not independent witnesses.
- When recording a new result, update the existing canonical semantic object when authorized; append history or successor links rather than erasing previous failures. Do not infer a current head from recency alone. Resolve conflicts with the authoritative owner or retain the ambiguity.
- Retrieve applicable cross-project methods by their assumptions and transfer conditions, not by superficial similarity. Preserve the original evidence scope and test a new application independently when necessary.
- Produce a compact decision-linked view, not a compulsory full archive recap. Use CACHE for bounded freshness and TRACE for execution provenance when those capabilities actually add value.

Return the useful recovered state, source and genealogy references, material conflicts, outstanding questions and next decision. Do not grant scientific authority by storage, retrieval or synthesis.
